# Few-Shot Example Ordering Effects on In-Context Learning — Research Report

## 1. Executive Summary

**Question.** How does the *order* of few-shot demonstrations affect in-context learning? We test this
across shot counts (K = 1–8), task types (sentiment, NLI, summarization) and model sizes
(Qwen2.5-Instruct 0.5B → 14B). We also ask which demo positions drive the model's output.

**Answer.** Order matters, but most of the specific hypothesis is **not supported**:

1. **Shot count.** Order variance does not rise up to 4-shot and then plateau. Measured without bias,
   it is already at its plateau at K = 2 and stays flat to K = 8. The naive best–worst *range* does
   show a "rise until 4-shot" shape, but that comes from the number of possible orders (2, 6, then
   8 sampled), not from the model.
2. **NLI vs sentiment gap.** The qualitative claim holds: NLI is more order-sensitive than sentiment.
   The thresholds mostly fail. The mean best–worst gap over 8 orders (K ≥ 4) is 5.9–13.3 points on
   MNLI and 2.3–6.8 on RTE (claim: >15), and 1.0–4.8 points on SST-2 (claim: <5). Individual
   worst-case demo sets do reach 21.7 points on MNLI and 15.5 points on SST-2, for the 0.5B model.
3. **Classification recency.** It is not a general rule. Causal label-flip probes find "last demo
   ≈ 2–3× first" in only 2 of 15 model×task cells (SST-2 at 1.5B, RTE at 7B). On NLI, models of
   1.5B–3B show the *opposite*: flipping the last demo's label moves predictions *away* from that
   label. 7B/14B on SST-2 weight early demos more, and 14B on NLI is roughly position-uniform.
4. **Generation primacy.** Refuted. For SAMSum summarization, a style marker has *more* influence
   when it sits in a later demo (first/last influence ratio 0.64–0.80 for 0.5B–3B). At 7B and 14B
   the effect is essentially position-independent (1.02–1.03 and 0.98).
5. **Model size.** Larger models are less order-sensitive on SST-2 and RTE (monotone; Spearman
   ρ = −1 over 5 sizes). The trend is non-monotone on MNLI (minimum at 7B). On SAMSum the trend
   points the other way: 14B is the most order-sensitive model and its order effects are the most
   reproducible. Caveat: identical-prompt reruns show that about a third of the SAMSum order variance is bf16
   numerical noise for most sizes, and 14B could not be re-run (§4.4b). Classification order
   effects are well above this noise floor.

**Practical implication.** For capable models (≥3B) on easy tasks like SST-2, order-induced
differences are about 1 point and are no larger than item-level noise. Choosing a "best order" there
is overfitting. On harder tasks (MNLI, and summarization at 14B), order effects are systematic and
reproducible across test items (split-half ρ ≈ 0.5–0.7). Order search or order-averaging can
matter there.

## 2. Research Question & Motivation

The hypothesis under test had five parts:

- **H1** Order variance increases with shot count up to 4-shot, then plateaus.
- **H2** Classification shows recency: the last example is 2–3× more influential than the first.
- **H3** Generation shows primacy.
- **H4** The best-to-worst order gap exceeds 15 points (accuracy) on NLI and stays below 5 on sentiment.
- **H5** Larger models are less order-sensitive.

Few-shot prompting is the default way practitioners adapt LLMs, yet demo order is usually an
uncontrolled variable. If order swings accuracy, few-shot results carry hidden variance, and
practitioners need to know when order search, calibration or order-averaging is worth the effort.

**Related work.** The resource-gathering stage was skipped for this workspace, so there was no
`literature_review.md`. Lu et al. (2022, *Fantastically Ordered Prompts*) showed large order
sensitivity for GPT-2/3 on classification, which did not shrink with scale. Zhao et al. (2021,
*Calibrate Before Use*) documented majority-label and recency bias in GPT-3. Liu et al. (2023, *Lost
in the Middle*) found U-shaped position effects in long contexts. Our contribution is a single
controlled protocol on a modern instruction-tuned model family. It (a) separates order variance from
the number of orders sampled, (b) measures per-position influence *causally* via interventions,
(c) treats classification and generation the same way, and (d) checks whether "good orders"
reproduce across test items.

## 3. Experimental Setup

**Models.** Qwen2.5-Instruct at 0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B and 14B (bf16), run locally with vLLM 0.31
(torch 2.13, CUDA 13) on one shared NVIDIA RTX A6000 (48 GB). No API keys were available on the
host. One model family keeps size as the only systematic difference. The family is from 2024; frontier
API models were not tested.

**Decoding.** Deterministic throughout. For classification, the next token is restricted to the
label tokens (`allowed_token_ids`, `logprobs_mode=processed_logprobs`), which gives a log-softmax
over the label set; the prediction is the argmax. For generation: greedy decoding, max 80 new
tokens, stop at newline. Because decoding is deterministic, **all variance across orders is caused
by order**.

**Prompts.** Plain-text completion format, no chat template. Example (RTE, K = 2):
```
Decide whether the premise entails the hypothesis. Answer yes or no.

Premise: ...
Hypothesis: ...
Answer: no

Premise: ...
Hypothesis: ...
Answer: yes

Premise: <test>
Hypothesis: <test>
Answer:
```
The other templates are in `src/tasks.py`:
- SST-2: `Review: … / Sentiment: positive|negative`.
- MNLI: `yes|maybe|no`, with the header defining these as entailment/neutral/contradiction.
- SAMSum: `Dialogue: … / Summary: …`, under the header "Summarize each dialogue in one or two sentences."

**Data.**

| Task | Type | Demo pool (train, length-filtered) | Test items |
|---|---|---|---|
| SST-2 | sentiment, 2-way | 41,584 | 200 (val, balanced) |
| RTE | NLI, 2-way | 1,830 | 200 (val) |
| MNLI-matched | NLI, 3-way | 327,814 | 201 (val, balanced) |
| SAMSum | dialogue summarization | 7,640 | 80 (test) |

**Design (shared by every model, seed 42).**
- **Classification:** K ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8}. For each K, 10 random demo sets (labels not forced to
  balance, so label positions vary). Each set is evaluated under min(K!, 8) distinct orders: all
  orders for K ≤ 3, 8 random orders for K ≥ 4.
- **Extension sets:** 10 more demo sets per K (seed 1042) for the 0.5B, 1.5B and 3B models.
  These were skipped for 7B/14B to save GPU time on the shared host.
- **SAMSum:** K ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6}, 6 sets, min(K!, 6) orders, 80 test dialogues.
- **Baseline:** zero-shot (K = 0).
- **Total:** about 2.36 M scored prompts and generations.

**Metrics.**
- Accuracy for classification; ROUGE-L F1 (stemmed) for SAMSum.
- *Order sensitivity* is computed per demo set, then averaged over sets:
  - SD across orders;
  - range (best − worst);
  - **pairwise gap**, E|m(π) − m(π′)| over order pairs. Its expectation does not depend on how many
    orders were sampled, so unlike the range it can be compared across K;
  - prediction **flip rate** between two orders.
- *Reliability of order effects:* split-half Spearman correlation of per-order scores across two
  random halves of the test items (20 splits). This is compared with a **null** that shuffles order
  labels independently within each test item, which keeps item-level variability but removes any
  order-level structure.
- *Position influence*, causal probes (`src/run_probe.py`). Both use demo sets 0–9 and the first
  2 orders of each:
  - **Label-flip (classification):** at K ∈ {4, 8}, flip the label of the demo at position i,
    keeping all else fixed. Influence_i = mean increase in log P(flipped-to label) on the test items.
  - **Style marker (SAMSum):** at K ∈ {4, 6}, rewrite demo i's summary to start with "In short, ".
    Influence_i = increase in log P(output starts with " In short,") relative to a no-marker
    control.
  - The 14B probe was run only at K = 8 (classification) and K = 6 (SAMSum) to save time.
- 95% CIs come from bootstrap resampling of demo sets (2,000 resamples).

**Interpretation.**

### 4.4 Are order effects real or item-level noise? (reliability analysis, K ≥ 3)

| Task | 0.5B | 1.5B | 3B | 7B | 14B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **split-half ρ of per-order scores** | | | | | |
| MNLI | 0.56 | 0.72 | 0.53 | 0.47 | 0.58 |
| RTE | 0.45 | 0.47 | 0.18 | 0.10 | 0.23 |
| SST-2 | 0.38 | 0.30 | −0.04 | −0.07 | 0.07 |
| SAMSum | 0.26 | 0.22 | 0.47 | 0.38 | 0.67 |
| **observed range / null range** | | | | | |
| MNLI | 2.08 | 2.83 | 1.83 | 1.70 | 2.07 |
| RTE | 1.79 | 1.60 | 1.22 | 1.11 | 1.23 |
| SST-2 | 1.64 | 1.41 | 1.00 | 0.89 | 0.98 |
| SAMSum | 1.22 | 1.39 | 1.80 | 1.54 | 2.67 |

- **Where order effects are systematic:** MNLI for all sizes, and SAMSum for ≥3B (strongest at
  14B). There, an order that is good on one half of the test items tends to be good on the other
  half, and the observed range is about 2× what item-level noise produces.
- **Where they are noise:** SST-2 for ≥3B, and RTE for ≥3B to a lesser degree. The "best" and
  "worst" orders are indistinguishable from re-shuffled noise (ratio ≈ 1, ρ ≈ 0). This qualifies
  every "best–worst gap" number above. On a 200-item test set, about 1–2 points of range appear
  even when order has no systematic effect.

### 4.4b Numerical noise floor (identical prompts re-run)

vLLM bf16 inference is not bit-deterministic across batch compositions. To check that "order
effects" are not just this numerical jitter, we re-ran the exact same prompts (K = 4, demo sets 0–2,
all 8 orders) for each model and compared the two runs (`src/run_noise_floor.sh`,
`src/run_noise_floor_large.sh`, table `results/tables/noise_floor.csv`).
*noise share* = E[(rerun diff)²] / E[(order diff)²]: the fraction of the order-induced squared
score differences that identical-prompt reruns also produce.

| Model | Task | Pred. flips, rerun (%) | Pred. flips, order (%) | Mean abs gap, rerun (pts) | Mean pairwise order gap (pts) | Noise share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5B | MNLI / RTE / SST-2 | 2.9 / 2.4 / 0.3 | 17.7 / 7.0 / 10.8 | 0.98 / 0.96 / 0.21 | 3.89 / 2.21 / 6.49 | 0.06 / 0.17 / 0.00 |
| 1.5B | MNLI / RTE / SST-2 | 1.3 / 0.9 / 0.1 | 9.1 / 3.8 / 2.2 | 0.52 / 0.56 / 0.10 | 4.52 / 1.38 / 1.10 | 0.02 / 0.23 / 0.03 |
| 3B | MNLI / RTE / SST-2 | 0.8 / 0.3 / 0.1 | 10.4 / 2.3 / 3.4 | 0.54 / 0.17 / 0.08 | 3.15 / 0.72 / 0.69 | 0.04 / 0.07 / 0.06 |
| 7B | MNLI / RTE / SST-2 | 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.0 | 5.8 / 2.9 / 1.0 | 0.25 / 0.25 / 0.00 | 1.85 / 1.20 / 0.42 | 0.03 / 0.06 / 0.00 |
| 0.5B / 1.5B / 3B / 7B | SAMSum (ROUGE-L) | 53 / 35 / 38 / 35 (outputs differ) | — | 0.47 / 0.53 / 0.40 / 0.51 | 0.84 / 0.80 / 2.31 / 0.90 | 0.30 / 0.42 / 0.03 / 0.35 |

- **Classification order effects are real, not numerical artifacts.** Reruns flip 0–3% of
  predictions, while order changes flip 1–18%. Numerical noise accounts for ≤ 6% of order variance
  in 10 of 12 cells, with a maximum of 23% (RTE 1.5B).
- **For generation, numerical noise is a large confound.** About 35–53% of greedy summaries
  differ textually between identical runs. For 0.5B, 1.5B and 7B, 30–42% of the ROUGE-L order
  variance at K = 4 is reproduced by reruns alone. Only 3B (noise share 0.03) shows clearly
  above-noise order effects at K = 4. This weakens the SAMSum sensitivity numbers in §4.2 and §4.7
  for those sizes. The marker-copy probe (§4.6) is less affected because it uses many
  paired comparisons.
- **14B could not be re-run.** Its bf16 weights (~30 GB) did not fit in the free memory on the
  shared GPUs (≤ 23 GB) during this session. So the SAMSum claim that "14B is the most
  order-sensitive" has no noise-floor check. Its high split-half reliability (ρ = 0.67, §4.4)
  argues that the effect is systematic, since numerical noise should not be consistent across
  halves of the test items.

![label flip influence](figures/fig4b_cls_labelflip_influence.png)

**Findings.**
- **"Last ≈ 2–3× first" (H2) holds in only two of 15 model×task cells (K = 8):** SST-2 with 1.5B
  (2.4×, CI excludes 1) and RTE with 7B (1.8× [1.27, 2.44]; 2.2× at K = 4). At K = 4, 1.5B SST-2
  gives 1.5× [0.99, 2.6]. For 0.5B on SST-2, influence rises steeply toward the end (0.73 at the last
  position) but the first position is slightly *negative*, so a ratio is meaningless; this is
  recency in shape. Several cells point the other way: SST-2 7B (0.49×) and 14B (0.57×) weight
  *early* demos more.
- **NLI shows anti-recency for 1.5B and 3B.** Flipping the *last* demo's label lowers the
  probability of that label (e.g. 3B MNLI K = 4: −0.79 nats for the last position vs +0.61 for the
  first). The prediction-level probe agrees: −4.2 percentage points toward the flipped label for 3B
  MNLI at K = 8. One possible mechanism is that when the demo closest to the test item looks
  *inconsistent*, the model contrasts away from it rather than copying it. We did not test this.
- **Unmanipulated prompts agree.** How often the prediction matches the label of the demo at each
  position (`cls_label_agreement_by_position.csv`): for 3B MNLI at K = 8, 36.4% at position 1 vs
  30.8% at position 8 (chance ≈ 33%). For 14B, 36.8% vs 31.8%. For 0.5B SST-2, 47.8% vs 52.8%, the
  only clear recency case.
- **14B is roughly position-uniform** on RTE and MNLI, and mildly primacy-weighted on SST-2.
- **Verdict:** H2 is **not supported** as a general claim. Recency appears in a few model×task cells
  (small models on SST-2, 7B on RTE). Mid-size models on NLI show primacy or anti-recency, and the
  larger models on SST-2 show mild primacy. The direction of the position effect depends on both
  model and task.
- An observational regression estimate on the natural, unmanipulated prompts is also provided
  (`cls_position_influence.csv`, Fig. `fig4_cls_position_influence_regression.png`). Its absolute
  per-position values are poorly identified: they come mostly from label-composition differences
  between only 10–20 demo sets, which are confounded with demo content. Only within-set
  last-minus-first contrasts are clean, so we treat the causal probe as primary.

![marker influence](figures/fig5b_gen_marker_influence.png)

- **Generation shows recency, not primacy, for small models.** Influence rises monotonically with
  position for 0.5B–3B (first/last 0.64–0.80, CIs exclude 1). At 7B it is essentially flat, with a

  (`gen_copy_by_position.csv`, Fig. `fig6_gen_copy_by_position.png`) — is small (<1.5% of output
  words) and noisy, with no consistent primacy.

| Task | Pairwise gap, 0.5B → 14B | Spearman ρ (p) |
|---|---|---|
| SST-2 | 2.10 → 1.01 → 0.76 → 0.44 → 0.39 | −1.0 (<.001) |
| RTE | 2.96 → 2.37 → 1.16 → 1.00 → 0.92 | −1.0 (<.001) |
| MNLI | 5.14 → 4.27 → 2.92 → 2.37 → 3.60 | −0.7 (0.19) |
| SAMSum, ROUGE-L | 0.96 → 1.28 → 1.45 → 1.20 → 2.29 | +0.7 (0.19) |

Prediction flip rates between two orders fall from 4.6% to 0.6% (SST-2), 9.1% to 1.9% (RTE) and
22% to 10% (MNLI).

**H5 is supported for classification on SST-2 and RTE, but not universally.** On MNLI, 14B is more
sensitive than 7B. On generation, sensitivity *grows* with size: 14B's summaries change more with
order, and reproducibly so (split-half ρ = 0.67). Only 5 sizes from one family were tested, so the
p-values for the trends are necessarily coarse.

## 5. Discussion

- **What survives from the hypothesis.**
  - NLI is more order-sensitive than sentiment.
  - Bigger models are usually less order-sensitive on classification.
  - Order variance is flat at higher K (the "plateau").
- **What does not survive.**
  - The specific 4-shot threshold. With an unbiased metric the plateau is reached at K = 2; the
    best–worst range only *looks* like it rises until 4-shot because more orders become available
    as K grows (2, then 6, then 8 sampled). Studies that report ranges over K! or a fixed small
    number of permutations should be read with this in mind.
  - The 15 / 5 point thresholds.
  - The recency/primacy split. Recency appears where it was not predicted (generation) and
    fails where it was (NLI).
- **Relation to prior work.**
  - Zhao et al.'s recency bias, found in GPT-3 *base* models on sentiment-like tasks, shows up here
    only in the smallest instruction-tuned models on SST-2. Instruction tuning and scale appear to
    remove it.
  - Lu et al. reported that order sensitivity does not vanish with scale. We find it shrinks
    substantially on easy tasks but persists on MNLI, and grows for generation at 14B.
- **The most practically useful finding is the reliability analysis.** Whether a "best order"
  exists depends on task and model. For strong models on easy tasks, order differences are
  indistinguishable from item-level noise, so tuning order on a dev set would overfit. Where effects
  are systematic (MNLI; generation at ≥3B), averaging over orders or searching for an order is
  justified.
- **Error analysis notes.**
  - 0.5B summaries usually run to the 80-token cap with repetitive text (median 67 words vs 24 for
    14B), which explains its low, flat ROUGE-L.
  - On MNLI, 14B accuracy *drops* below zero-shot at K = 2–4 (77–78 vs 80) and recovers at K = 8.
    Demonstrations can hurt strong models when few and unbalanced.
  - The anti-recency effect on NLI is concentrated in the final position: the influence profile is
    flat for earlier positions, then turns negative (Fig. 4b).

## 6. Limitations

- **One model family** (Qwen2.5-Instruct), deterministic decoding, plain-text prompts, one template
  per task. Other families or chat-formatted prompts may behave differently. No frontier API
  models were available on the host.
- **Test sets** have 200 items (classification) and 80 (SAMSum), so per-order accuracy has roughly
  ±3 points of item-level noise. Section 4.4 quantifies this.
- **Uneven demo-set counts.** 10 demo sets per K (20 for ≤3B). Orders per set were capped at 8, so
  best–worst ranges are lower bounds of the true K!-order range (8! = 40,320).
- **Reduced probes for the largest models.** The 14B probe used only K = 8 (classification) and
  K = 6 (SAMSum). 7B and 14B did not get the 10 extension demo sets (GPU time on a shared host).
- **The generation probe** measures one kind of influence (copying a surface-style marker). Primacy
  in content or ordering of facts was not tested. SAMSum is the only generation task.
- **Contamination.** SST-2, GLUE and SAMSum are public and likely in the pretraining data. This
  affects absolute accuracy more than order effects.
- **Non-deterministic inference.** vLLM bf16 outputs vary between identical runs. This is
  negligible for classification but accounts for about a third of the measured SAMSum order
  variance for most sizes (§4.4b). There is no 14B noise-floor run.
- **Size trends** rest on 5 points, so ρ = ±0.7 is not significant.

## 7. Conclusions & Next Steps

Demo order changes few-shot performance by about 1–13 points of best–worst range, depending
strongly on task and model. Variance plateaus already at 2 shots. Position influence is
task-dependent rather than a universal recency (classification) / primacy (generation) split: we see
recency for small models on sentiment and for generation, and primacy or anti-recency for mid-size
models on NLI. Scale reduces order sensitivity for simple classification but not for NLI or
summarization. Order effects on easy tasks with capable models are mostly noise.

**Follow-ups.**
1. Replicate on other families (Llama-3.x, Gemma, an API model) and with chat templates.
2. Investigate the NLI anti-recency mechanism: is it contrast from an inconsistent nearest
   demonstration? Try flipping to *correct* vs *incorrect* labels.
3. Larger test sets and full permutation enumeration for K ≤ 5, to estimate true worst cases.
4. Content-level primacy probes for generation (e.g. which demo's entities or structure get copied).
5. Test whether order-averaging (ensembling over orders) recovers the systematic MNLI losses.

# Planning: Few-Shot Example Ordering Effects on In-Context Learning

## Motivation & Novelty Assessment

### Why This Research Matters
Few-shot prompting is the default way practitioners adapt LLMs, yet the *order* of demonstrations is an
uncontrolled nuisance variable. If ordering can swing accuracy by double digits, reported few-shot numbers
are partly noise and prompt designers need order-robust practices. Knowing *when* (task type, shot count,
model size) ordering matters tells practitioners where to invest in order search or calibration.

### Gap in Existing Work
Note: the resource-finder stage was skipped for this workspace (no `literature_review.md`, `papers/`,
`code/`), so this assessment relies on well-known prior work:
- Lu et al. 2022 ("Fantastically Ordered Prompts") — order sensitivity for GPT-2/GPT-3 on classification;
  large gaps, not reduced by scale, but on old base models and mostly single shot counts.
- Zhao et al. 2021 ("Calibrate Before Use") — recency bias (majority/last label) for GPT-3.
- Liu et al. 2023 ("Lost in the Middle") — U-shaped position effects in long-context retrieval/QA.
These do not jointly measure (a) how order variance scales with shot count, (b) *per-position influence*
quantitatively (first vs last), (c) classification vs generation under one protocol, and (d) a clean
within-family size sweep on modern instruction-tuned models.

### Our Novel Contribution
A single controlled protocol on the Qwen2.5-Instruct family (0.5B→14B) that measures, for sentiment
(SST-2), NLI (RTE, MNLI), and summarization (SAMSum): order variance vs. K∈{1..8}, best–worst order gaps,
and a regression-based per-position influence estimate that directly tests "last example 2–3× more
influential than first" (classification) and "primacy dominates" (generation).

### Experiment Justification
- Exp 1 (shot-count sweep, classification): needed for the "variance rises until 4-shot then plateaus" claim
  and the NLI (>15%) vs sentiment (<5%) gap claim.
- Exp 2 (position-influence regression, re-using Exp 1 prompts): tests recency for classification.
- Exp 3 (SAMSum generation sweep): tests primacy for generation (output length / lexical copying traced
  to each demo position) and generation order variance.
- Exp 4 (model-size sweep, all above across 5 sizes): tests "larger models less order-sensitive".

## Research Question
How does the order of few-shot demonstrations affect ICL performance as a function of shot count, task
type, and model size, and which demo positions dominate the model's output?

## Hypothesis Decomposition
- H1: Across-order SD of accuracy increases from K=1 to K=4, then is flat (K=4,6,8 not different).
- H2: Classification: influence of last demo ≥2× influence of first demo (recency).
- H3: Generation: influence of first demo > influence of last demo (primacy).
- H4: Best–worst order gap >15 points on NLI (RTE/MNLI), <5 points on SST-2.
- H5: Order sensitivity (SD, gap) decreases with parameter count.

## Proposed Methodology

### Approach
Real models, run locally with vLLM on one RTX A6000 (no API keys available on this host).
Models: Qwen2.5-{0.5B,1.5B,3B,7B,14B}-Instruct (same family/data → cleanest size comparison).
Plain-text completion prompts (no chat template), greedy / argmax decoding (deterministic, so all
variance across orders is caused by order).

### Experimental Steps
1. Fixed test sets: SST-2 val (200, balanced), RTE val (200), MNLI-matched val (201, balanced).
2. For each K∈{1,2,3,4,6,8}: sample S=10 demo sets from train (random labels, not forced balanced so
   label positions vary); for each set evaluate P=min(K!,8) distinct permutations on all test items.
3. Classification scoring: next-token log-probs restricted to the label words; prediction = argmax.
4. SAMSum: K∈{1,2,3,4,6}, S=6 sets, P=min(K!,6) orders, N=80 test dialogues; greedy, ≤80 tokens.
5. Repeat for all five models.

### Baselines
Zero-shot (K=0) per task/model; majority-class; across-demo-set variance (to contextualize order variance
against "which examples" variance).

### Evaluation Metrics
- Accuracy (classification), ROUGE-L F1 (generation).
- Order sensitivity per demo set: SD across orders; range (best−worst); and *expected pairwise gap*
  E|acc(π)−acc(π')| (unbiased w.r.t. number of permutations sampled, comparable across K).
- Position influence β_i: regression of the model's label log-prob score on "demo at position i has
  label c" with test-item × class fixed effects; for generation, regression of output length on
  demo-i summary length (test FE) and lexical copying overlap with demo i.

### Statistical Analysis Plan
Bootstrap CIs over demo sets (resampling sets); Mann–Whitney / Welch tests for K comparisons;
cluster-robust (by prompt) SEs for β; Spearman correlation for size trend; α=0.05, Holm correction
within each hypothesis family.

## Expected Outcomes
Support: SD rises K=1→4 then flat; β_last/β_first ∈[2,3]; generation β_first>β_last; NLI gap>15,
SST-2 gap<5; Spearman(size, SD)<0. Any deviation is reported as refutation of that sub-claim.

## Timeline and Milestones
Setup 30 min; code + pilot 45 min; runs ~2–3 h GPU; analysis 45 min; write-up 30 min.

## Potential Challenges
- Shared GPU may become busy → keep models ≤14B, checkpoint per model/task.
- Label tokens missing from top-k logprobs → request top-20 and floor-fill; log coverage.
- Small K!: K=2 only has 2 orders, K=3 has 6 → use pairwise-gap metric that is unbiased in P.
- Test-set sampling noise in accuracy (N=200 → SE≈3.5pt) inflates order SD slightly; report it.

## Success Criteria
All five hypotheses receive a clear supported/refuted verdict with CIs, from real model outputs.
